Stakeholders, Personas & the Wants↔Needs Map

Who this is really for, what they say they want, what the job actually needs to be — and the fluff we deliberately refused to build. The product's scope is the disciplined result of this map, not a feature wishlist.
The lens: a want is what a user asks for; a need is the job that, done right, makes them successful. Great products are defined as much by the wants they decline as the needs they serve. Every row below traces a real need to a shipped capability — and every declined item names why.

4What we deliberately refused to build — and why

The fluff, named. Each is a real request we heard (or the market expects) and consciously cut, because serving it would break a need above.
✕ AI that decides money
→ Deterministic gates; a human clears every flag
The instant a model can approve a dollar, Ana can't sign the audit and Denise carries the blame. Non-negotiable.
✕ Silent auto-approve
→ Fail-closed, named reason, review queue
"No history for this part" must stop and ask a person — never quietly pass. A missed overcharge is the whole failure mode.
✕ 40-widget BI dashboard
→ "What needs attention" first; one traceable number
Ray reads it from a truck. Vanity charts bury the one dollar that matters.
✕ "Learns your patterns" black box
→ Taught rules with mandatory source + evidence
A rule you can't point at can't be defended to a vendor or an auditor. If it can't cite its source, it can't veto.
✕ Cloud-only, send scans out
→ On-device OCR + local store + portable receipts
Financial docs shouldn't leave the building to be read. Governance is a need, not a nice-to-have.
✕ ERP / QuickBooks replacement
→ Fits the existing flow (Excel in, CSV/email/Slack out)
Nobody's ripping out their books for this. It earns trust by slotting in, not by demanding a migration.
